# Blackwood story continuity

The chapel repeater spreads an unidentified carrier beneath familiar voices and recorded messages. Prolonged exposure first damages sleep and memory, then changes the body. The three chapel creatures are transformed living residents. The shrouded house and mausoleum-tunnel creatures are older dead bodies reanimated by sustained exposure to the same frequency; this does not restore their former lives or establish whether consciousness remains. What produced the carrier after the storm remains unknown.

## Timeline

- In 1891, mill families funded the hilltop church above the creek's flood line. Its nave served as a refuge during floods. The county added the civil-defense repeater decades later; the church was a place of safety long before the signal.
- Twelve years ago, the mill closed. Most families moved away over the following years. Boarded businesses, old cars, damaged fences, and long-neglected rooms come from this decline. Sebus Hardware is one of the long-shuttered storefronts. A handful of families stayed, supported by the general store, clinic, and repair shop. John and Ada lived in an occupied room at Saint George Hotel.
- Nine years ago, the old Victorian house's occupants left. It was the mill superintendent's residence, separate from John and Ada's hotel room. Its clearance notice remains downstairs. Pat inspected it after the recent storm and found fresh bare footprints leading into the cellar; he does not know who made them.
- One month before evacuation, a storm damages the civil-defense repeater beneath the chapel. The storm also worsens existing roof damage and brings down rotten ceilings. A low tone begins in the relayed signal. Its buried line feeds the town station and porch speaker. The inspection is never completed.
- Over the following weeks, residents near running sets and loudspeakers lose sleep, hear borrowed voices, forget things and become confused or violent. Severe cases develop locked jaws and distorted hands. Exposure varies; early symptoms ease away from the signal. Ordinary radio is not inherently infectious.
- Three weeks before evacuation, John inspects the chapel cellar, before creatures reach its grounds. The station still works, so he leaves the spare county tuner isolated and the rifle with the emergency supplies. His dated equipment-check sheet remains on the cellar bench.
- One week before evacuation, caretaker Jonah, who sleeps near the continuously running rack, changes and retreats into the woods. Father Bellamy witnesses it. His maintenance book admits that keeping the warning speaker on spread the carrier.
- Three nights before play, the clinic evacuates the last remaining families. Most of the town has already been empty for years. Ruth, Ada and Father Bellamy leave on the final 01:40 southbound bus. John stays to secure the equipment; Aubrey and Pat search for the missing.
- At 02:10, Edith and Silas, two advanced patients who refuse the bus, walk uphill. Aubrey recognizes Edith's clinic bracelet on a changing wrist. John later records Jonah's work coat and Silas's splinted hand. These are the three creatures around the chapel.
- At 02:17, the loudspeaker plays a recorded bus horn. Pat reopens the barrier; no vehicle returns.
- At 02:20, Pat broadcasts "Three still here. Road open." This refers to Pat, Aubrey and John, the three remaining untransformed residents. The repeater later loops it. The Population 3 sign does not count the three transformed missing residents or the player.
- Over the following three days, the independently powered chapel speaker repeats its horn. Pat recognizes it as a recording during lucid moments, but repeated exposure disrupts his memory: attempts to close the barrier end in missing hours and renewed waiting for the nonexistent bus. Tonight Aubrey makes him compare three dated log entries; he realizes how much time has passed, with the road still open as the player arrives. This is an early mental effect of the carrier, not an instant physical transformation.
- After the evacuation, John removes the town coil. That stops relayed town broadcasts, but the independently powered chapel rack remains active. The hill is the remaining concentration of the carrier. On a later attempt to return for the tuner and rifle, the creatures drive him back down the hill. His observations of the transformed residents and their return after gunfire belong to his later workshop log, not the earlier cellar inspection.
- John observes that a shot stops a creature temporarily; while the carrier runs, it moves again about two minutes later. This explains the gameplay respawn without implying gunfire cures the transformation.
- Tonight, the player follows an undated detour sheet collected at a fuel stop, swerves around a figure and crashes outside town. Brief exposure is not instant transformation. No hidden infection timer is imposed on the player.

## Evidence and motivations

Nine existing records distribute the explanation: the clinic charts progressive symptoms; Ada documents John's early memory trouble and improvement away from the set; the store records wider confusion; the vestry book names the first transformation; John's later workshop log identifies the three chapel creatures and explains their return; the earlier cellar check documents the isolated equipment and routing. Aubrey's conversation responds to the clinic evidence. The investigation remains optional and all notes remain available in the journal.

Pat regrets reopening the barrier and losing three days to a signal-induced memory loop that kept him from securing it again. Aubrey stays to search for her patients. John stays to isolate the equipment, then cannot get past the creatures to retrieve the tuner. Ada and Father Bellamy leave on the final bus. Evacuation was a response to weeks of symptoms, not the cause of the pre-existing decay.

## Radio logic and endings

Optional questions are distributed among the survivors: Pat covers Blackwood's decline and the Victorian house, Aubrey describes the tone's symptoms and Edith and Silas, and John explains the hilltop church equipment and his promise to Ada. Each has two lore topics and progress-aware advice, with separate first greetings and journal attribution. Pat's mausoleum-key handoff retains priority. Evidence deepens their answers without establishing the carrier's origin; conversation notes never count as physical evidence or unlock quest progress.

The station needs the workshop coil and isolated cellar tuning unit. Repair alone restores power; it does not reroute the antenna. The physical desk BYPASS switch selects AMBER (chapel repeater, carrying the tone) or GREEN (direct county, avoiding it). The player must physically shut down the chapel rack at MAIN ISOLATOR before a final transmission is possible. The rack hum and compromised radio noise stop; dead chapel creatures can no longer respawn. With the repeater off, AMBER is a dead route. Select GREEN and hold E at the main receiver to call. Reading never toggles hardware.

- AMBER: cannot complete the game. The now-unpowered repeater cannot route the call; select GREEN.
- GREEN: NO SIRENS. County acknowledges a quiet approach from Old Mill Road. The player reports surviving residents and switches the set off. The ending confirms the chapel carrier has been silenced and rescue contact established. It is not a cure or a complete explanation of the carrier's origin.

Sirens attract the transformed residents through ordinary sound; they are not the source of the original illness. The green channel avoids sending the compromised repeater's carrier to responders. Documents, survivor count and evidence percentage never gate rescue; physical repeater shutdown and direct-county routing do.

## Chapel assets

The hilltop chapel uses a single continuous shell with the user's local Flooded Grounds bell tower, door prefab and pews. The tower is cut against the roof slopes; the original complete building sections are not stacked inside the nave. Entrance and cellar door leaves are fitted to their frames. Original UVs and reduced source atlases are retained with flat shading, matte materials and bounded mesh budgets. Established collision and quest interactions remain in place: shut entrance doors, clear central aisle, side lectern, keyed cellar, tuning unit and service rifle.

Ada's letter is an optional delivery quest. John asks for it during his Ada conversation; finding it first also works. Collection removes the physical hotel letter and adds one protected backpack item. Giving it to living John consumes that item, plays his reaction and marks the side quest complete. Its transcript remains evidence in the journal. This never gates rescue.

The Victorian cellar foundation has been breached from outside. A short earthen burrow, inward-scattered rubble, claw gouges and snagged burial cloth suggest the reanimated dead dug into the house and reached its rooms via the cellar stairs. A visible solid collapse seals the burrow against player passage. Aubrey's later entry in the Saint George maintenance ledger describes coffins forced open from inside beneath the cemetery; her patient conversation distinguishes reanimation from the living residents' gradual transformation. The cemetery reaper and workshop creature remain unassigned by this explanation.

Radio guidance follows each survivor's perspective at every late quest stage: Pat emphasizes safe routes and verified rescue contact, Aubrey speaks in first person about her records and medical needs, and John explains power isolation and routing. They retain the same hardware requirements without sharing identical speeches.

During evacuation, confused residents entered the station and removed equipment. John asked Pat to secure the spare antenna coupler. Bellamy locked the chapel cellar to keep residents away from its running rack and handed its key to Pat before boarding the bus. Pat temporarily cached both items on a shelf in the dry Whitcomb family vault, whose intact iron door and lock he controlled. He intended to retrieve them once the bus cleared the road, but noises beneath the cemetery kept him away. This predates discovery of the reanimated dead below the vault. The cache is on the shelf, not in a coffin; it was never intended as permanent emergency storage.
